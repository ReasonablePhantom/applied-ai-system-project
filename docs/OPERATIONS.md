# Operating Rules

## Altitude Limit

A small unmanned aircraft may not be flown higher than **400 feet above
ground level (AGL)**, unless it remains within a 400-foot radius of a
structure and does not fly higher than 400 feet above the structure's
immediate uppermost limit. Within controlled airspace, an authorized
altitude from LAANC or DroneZone may further limit the maximum altitude
below 400 feet at a given location.

## Visual Line of Sight (VLOS)

The remote pilot in command (or a visual observer working with the remote
pilot) must be able to see the unmanned aircraft at all times during flight,
using unaided vision (corrective lenses/sunglasses are allowed). The
aircraft must be close enough for the remote pilot to know its location,
attitude, altitude, and direction of flight, and to see and avoid other
aircraft. First-person-view (FPV) goggles alone do not satisfy the VLOS
requirement unless a visual observer maintains direct VLOS in coordination
with the remote pilot.

## Daylight and Civil Twilight Operations

Operations are permitted during daylight, or during civil twilight (30
minutes before official sunrise and 30 minutes after official sunset, local
time), provided the small unmanned aircraft has lighted anti-collision
lighting visible for at least 3 statute miles that is capable of a flash
rate sufficient to avoid a collision hazard.

## Groundspeed

Maximum groundspeed is **100 mph (87 knots)**.

## Operations Over People and Moving Vehicles

Flying over people is restricted by category:

- **Category 1**: aircraft weighing 0.55 lbs or less, or meeting specific
  injury-threshold criteria, with no exposed rotating parts that could
  lacerate skin.
- **Category 2/3**: aircraft that meet specific means-of-compliance and
  injury-severity requirements declared by the manufacturer, with
  operational limitations (e.g., no sustained hover over open-air
  assemblies for Category 3).
- **Category 4**: aircraft that have an airworthiness certificate and are
  operated in accordance with the operating limitations in the approved
  Flight Manual.

An aircraft that does not meet one of these categories may not be operated
over a person who is not directly participating in the operation and is not
under a covered structure or inside a stationary vehicle.

Operating over a moving vehicle is only permitted (a) over people located
inside a stationary vehicle, or within a moving vehicle when the operation
meets the Category 1–4 requirements above, or (b) when the aircraft remains
within or transits over a closed- or restricted-access site where all
people are on notice.

## Right-of-Way Rules

A small unmanned aircraft must yield the right-of-way to all other aircraft,
manned or unmanned, and to launch/recovery operations. The remote pilot must
see and avoid other aircraft, and must not operate in a manner that creates
a collision hazard.

## No Operations From a Moving Vehicle or Aircraft

A remote pilot may not operate a small unmanned aircraft from a moving
vehicle or moving aircraft, unless the operation is over a sparsely
populated area and does not involve the transportation of property for
compensation or hire.

## Key Takeaways

- Max altitude: 400 ft AGL (or 400 ft above a nearby structure).
- Must maintain VLOS at all times.
- Daylight or civil twilight with anti-collision lighting.
- Max groundspeed: 100 mph / 87 knots.
- Flying over people requires meeting Category 1–4 criteria.
- Must always yield right-of-way to other aircraft.
