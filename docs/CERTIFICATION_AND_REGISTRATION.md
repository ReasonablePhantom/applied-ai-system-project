# Remote Pilot Certification and Aircraft Registration

## Remote Pilot Certificate

To operate a small unmanned aircraft under Part 107, a person must hold a
Remote Pilot Certificate with a small UAS rating, issued by the FAA. To
obtain one, an applicant must:

- Be at least 16 years old.
- Be able to read, speak, write, and understand English.
- Be in a physical and mental condition that would not interfere with safe
  operation.
- Pass the initial aeronautical knowledge test ("Part 107 test") at an
  FAA-approved knowledge testing center, covering topics such as
  regulations, airspace classification, weather, loading and performance,
  and operations.

The certificate must be renewed by passing a recurrent knowledge test (or
completing FAA-approved recurrent online training) every 24 calendar months.

## Aircraft Registration

Any small unmanned aircraft weighing more than **0.55 lbs (250 grams)** and
up to 55 lbs at takeoff, including everything on board or attached, must be
registered with the FAA before it is flown outdoors. Registration is done
online through the FAA DroneZone and is valid for a set renewal period.

Aircraft used for Part 107 (non-recreational) operations are registered
under the aircraft owner, and each physical aircraft used commercially must
carry its own registration number. The registration number must be marked
on the aircraft in a visible location.

## Weight Limit

Part 107 applies to small unmanned aircraft weighing **less than 55 lbs** at
takeoff, including everything on board or attached to the aircraft. Aircraft
at or above 55 lbs require different certification and are outside the
scope of the standard Part 107 rule.

## Accident Reporting

A remote pilot must report an accident to the FAA within 10 days if it
results in serious injury, loss of consciousness, or property damage of at
least $500, unless the aircraft is a Category 4 aircraft with an
airworthiness certificate.

## Preflight Requirements

Before each flight, the remote pilot in command must:

- Ensure the aircraft is in a condition for safe operation, inspecting for
  equipment damage or malfunction.
- Ensure all persons directly participating in the operation are informed
  of operating conditions, emergency procedures, contingency procedures,
  roles/responsibilities, and potential hazards.

## Key Takeaways

- Remote Pilot Certificate required; renew every 24 months.
- Register any aircraft over 0.55 lbs before flying outdoors.
- Part 107 covers aircraft under 55 lbs at takeoff.
- Report qualifying accidents to the FAA within 10 days.
