# Weather Minimums

## Visibility

A remote pilot must have a minimum flight visibility of **3 statute miles**
from the control station location. If visibility at the control station
falls below 3 statute miles, the operation is not permitted.

## Cloud Clearance

The small unmanned aircraft must remain clear of clouds by at least:

- **500 feet below** any cloud
- **2,000 feet horizontally** from any cloud

There is no cloud clearance requirement stated in feet above, because Part
107 operations are capped at 400 feet AGL and clouds at that altitude are
addressed by the horizontal and below-cloud minimums.

## Why These Minimums Exist

Visibility and cloud clearance minimums exist so the remote pilot (or a
visual observer) can maintain visual line of sight with the aircraft and so
the aircraft remains visible to, and can see and avoid, other aircraft that
may be operating nearby, including manned aircraft transitioning through
that airspace.

## Wind and Other Conditions

Part 107 does not set a specific maximum wind speed. The remote pilot is
responsible for assessing whether wind, precipitation, temperature, or other
conditions make the operation unsafe, and for aborting or declining to fly if
conditions exceed the aircraft's operating limitations or the pilot's
ability to maintain control.

## Checking Conditions Before Flight

Remote pilots should check current and forecast weather at the operating
location before every flight, including visibility, cloud ceilings, and
wind, and should reassess conditions throughout an operation since weather
can change while airborne.

## Key Takeaways

- Minimum flight visibility: 3 statute miles.
- Minimum cloud clearance: 500 feet below, 2,000 feet horizontal.
- No fixed wind limit in the regulation — pilot judgment governs.
