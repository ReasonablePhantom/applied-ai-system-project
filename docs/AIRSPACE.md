# Airspace Classification and Authorization

## Overview

Under 14 CFR Part 107, where a small unmanned aircraft (sUAS) may fly depends
on the class of airspace at that location. Airspace is either uncontrolled
(Class G) or controlled (Class B, C, D, or the surface area of Class E).

## Class G Airspace

Class G is uncontrolled airspace. A remote pilot may operate in Class G
airspace without prior air traffic control (ATC) authorization, subject to
the normal operating rules (altitude, visibility, right-of-way, etc.).

## Controlled Airspace (Class B, C, D, and surface Class E)

Operating in controlled airspace requires authorization before the flight.
There are two ways to get it:

1. **LAANC (Low Altitude Authorization and Notification Capability)** — an
   automated system that grants near-real-time authorization for
   pre-approved altitudes at a given location, via an FAA-approved app.
2. **DroneZone manual request** — for airspace or altitudes not covered by
   LAANC, a remote pilot must submit a request through the FAA DroneZone
   portal and wait for approval, which can take up to 90 days.

Flying in controlled airspace without authorization is a violation of Part
107, regardless of altitude.

## Airspace Maps

The FAA's UAS Facility Maps show the maximum altitude at each location within
controlled airspace for which LAANC authorization is generally available.
Requesting an altitude above the facility map value routes the request to
manual DroneZone review rather than instant LAANC approval.

## Special Use and Restricted Areas

Temporary Flight Restrictions (TFRs), restricted areas, prohibited areas,
national security UAS restrictions, and airspace around wildfires or other
emergencies can prohibit sUAS operations entirely, independent of airspace
class. A remote pilot must check for active TFRs before every flight.

## Key Takeaways

- Class G: no authorization required.
- Class B/C/D/E surface: authorization required (LAANC or DroneZone) before
  flight.
- Authorization is altitude- and location-specific — it does not carry over
  to a different location or a higher altitude than approved.
- TFRs and restricted areas can override normal airspace rules and must
  always be checked separately.
